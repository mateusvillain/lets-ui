## Purpose

The grid divides the available width into equal **columns**, separated by a **gutter**, inside a side **margin**. It keeps content aligned from one section to the next and from one screen to the next, and turns "how much width does this block take" into a simple decision: **how many columns**.

## Principles

- **Alignment above all.** Everything rests on the same columns, so nothing is crooked between sections.
- **Behavior before width.** A responsive layout is not the same layout shrunk: on mobile, what was side by side now stacks.
- **Mobile-first.** The grid starts with 4 columns and gains columns as the screen grows.
- **Content first.** The grid serves the content, it does not force it to fit into boxes.
- **Maximum width.** On very wide screens, content stops growing and centers, so lines of text do not get too long.

## Structure

| Piece | What it is | Token |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Columns** | Equal-width bands that blocks rest on. | `lui.brand.grid.column.*` |
| **Gutter** | The space between columns (the `gap`). | `lui.brand.grid.gap.*` |
| **Margin** | The breathing room between the grid and the screen edge. | `lui.brand.grid.margin.*` |
| **Maximum width** | Where the content stops growing and centers. | `lui.brand.grid.container.1xl` |
| **Breakpoints** | The widths at which columns, gutter and margin change. | `lui.brand.grid.breakpoint.*` |

### The full table

| Breakpoint | Viewport | Columns | Gutter | Side margin | Typical target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `1xs` (base) | up to 767px | 4 | 16px | 16px | Phone |
| `sm` | from 768px | 8 | 16px | 32px | Tablet, portrait |
| `md` | from 1024px | 8 | 16px | 40px | Tablet, landscape; small laptop |
| `lg` | from 1280px | 12 | 24px | 40px | Desktop |
| `1xl` | from 1440px | 12 | 24px | Centered at 1360px | Wide screens |

Twelve columns divide into 2, 3, 4 and 6; eight into 2 and 4; four into 2. That gives flexibility to split the page into any band.

## How to use

You choose how many columns each block takes in each band, and the grid converts that into width. A value declared at a breakpoint applies **from there up**, until another one replaces it.

```html
<lui-grid>
  <lui-grid-item col="full">Header</lui-grid-item>
  <lui-grid-item col="4" col-sm="4" col-lg="8">Content</lui-grid-item>
  <lui-grid-item col="4" col-sm="4" col-lg="4">Sidebar</lui-grid-item>
</lui-grid>
```

```html
<div class="grid">
  <div class="col-full">Header</div>
  <div class="col-4 col-sm-4 col-lg-8">Content</div>
  <div class="col-4 col-sm-4 col-lg-4">Sidebar</div>
</div>
```

In SCSS, use the `media-up($bp)` and `media-down($bp)` mixins. They are exact complements: no width, not even one made fractional by zoom, is left without a rule.

```scss
.panel {
  display: block;

  @include media-up(md) {
    display: flex;
  }
}
```

<Callout variant="warning">
  Watch out for the jump from 8 to 12 columns at `lg`: a `col-sm-4` block, which was half the row at `sm` and `md`, becomes a third at `lg` if it is not redeclared.
</Callout>

## Standard layouts

### Content page

Long text, such as an article or a help page. The text takes **8 of the 12 columns** on desktop, centered (or with a table of contents in the remaining 4), to keep the line close to 75 characters. On mobile, it takes all 4 columns.

| Band | Content | Table of contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phone | 4 | Above the content or collapsed |
| Tablet | 8 | Above the content |
| Desktop | 8 | 4 |

### Dashboard

Metric cards and charts. Metrics **3 or 4 per row** on desktop (`col-lg-3` or `col-lg-4`), **2 per row** on tablet (`col-sm-4`) and **1 per row** on mobile (`col-4`). Wide charts take the full row or 8 columns.

### Form

Forms are read top to bottom, in **one column**. Use at most 6 to 8 desktop columns, and put two fields side by side only when they form a unit (city and state, start and end date). Buttons are aligned to the edge of the form, not of the page.

### Detail

An item's page: the main content in **8 columns** and a metadata or actions panel in **4**. On tablet and mobile, the panel goes **above** the content if it has actions, or below if it is information only.

### Side navigation

The app shell with a fixed menu on the left. The navigation **is not part** of the page grid: use [Sidebar](../../components/layout/sidebar/index.mdx) (240px side panel and flexible content) or the product shell, and place the Grid **inside** the content area. Below `lg`, the navigation becomes a collapsible menu.

## Alignment and nesting

- Every block starts and ends **at a column edge**. Nothing sits at "half a column".
- Elements inside a block align to the block's inner edge, not to the grid.
- A Grid inside another **does not reapply** margin or maximum width: it divides the width of the parent cell. In the Web Component this is automatic (`flush`).
- Avoid more than **two levels** of nested grid. To align a few items inside a block, use a layout primitive.
- Use `grid--flush` for edge-to-edge bands (a banner with a background), keeping the columns inside.

## Grid, Flexbox or Stack?

| You need to | Use | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Structure the page into aligned columns | [Grid](../../components/layout/grid/index.mdx) | Two dimensions, tied to the brand tokens. |
| Limit the width and center | [Container](../../components/layout/container/index.mdx) | Maximum width by breakpoint, without columns. |
| Stack items vertically with uniform space | [Stack](../../components/layout/stack/index.mdx) | One dimension, space from the scale. |
| Items in a row that wrap (tags, buttons) | [Inline](../../components/layout/inline/index.mdx) | Natural wrapping, no breakpoint. |
| Fixed side panel and flexible content | [Sidebar](../../components/layout/sidebar/index.mdx) | Stacks on its own when space runs out. |
| Side-by-side items that stack below a width | [Switcher](../../components/layout/switcher/index.mdx) | Responsive to the container's space, not the viewport's. |
| Align an item in the center | [Center](../../components/layout/center/index.mdx) | One task, one tool. |
| Fine control of axis, order and alignment | [Flex](../../components/layout/flex/index.mdx) | Flexbox with the spacing scale. |

General rule: **Grid for the page, primitives for components.** If a card is using the page grid to align its icon with the text, it is the wrong tool.

## Accessibility

- **Visual order = DOM order.** Do not use `order` or grid placement to reorder content: the keyboard and the screen reader follow the HTML (WCAG 1.3.2 and 2.4.3).
- **Reflow.** At 320px wide (or 1280px at 400% zoom), content fits without horizontal scrolling (WCAG 1.4.10). The 4-column grid exists for this.
- **Zoom.** Browser zoom reduces the usable width and triggers smaller breakpoints. Test the layout at 200% zoom.
- **Landmark regions.** Use `header`, `nav`, `main` and `aside` for the layout regions; the grid does not replace semantic structure.

## Best practices

- Let the Grid apply columns, gutter and margin. Do not reproduce those values by hand.
- Think in columns, not pixels.
- Test at the **edges** of each breakpoint: just before and just after is where the layout breaks.
- Keep all text and controls inside the margin: nothing stuck to the screen edge.
- Prefer layouts that adapt to the content (Switcher, Sidebar, Inline) to per-breakpoint rules.

## Anti-patterns

<DosDonts variant="do" title="Structure that adapts">
  Two cards in a Switcher: side by side when there is room, stacked when there is not, without any media query.
</DosDonts>

<DosDonts variant="dont" title="A breakpoint to fix content">
  A media query at 900px just for a heading that overflows. The problem is the heading (or the container), and it will overflow again at another width.
</DosDonts>

- **Fixed pixel widths** on page blocks. They ignore the columns and break on smaller screens.
- **Grid inside grid inside grid.** Three levels of columns to position a button.
- **Reordering with CSS.** A side panel that appears first on screen, but last for the screen reader.
- **Custom breakpoints.** A media query at 900px creates a band that no other part of the system knows about.
