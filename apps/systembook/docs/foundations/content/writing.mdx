## Voice and tone

The product's **voice** is constant: clear, direct and respectful. The **tone** changes with the moment.

| Moment | Tone | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Common task | Neutral and objective | "Project created." |
| First use | Welcoming | "Create your first project to get started." |
| Success | Positive, without overdoing it | "Changes saved." |
| Error | Serious, helpful, blame-free | "Couldn't save. Check your connection and try again." |
| Destructive action | Clear and direct | "Delete 3 files? This can't be undone." |

Avoid humor in errors and in data loss.

## Principles

- **Be clear**: use simple words and short sentences.
- **Be specific**: say what happened and what to do.
- **Be brief**: cut everything that does not help the person decide or act.
- **Be consistent**: always use the same term for the same thing.
- **Talk to the person**: use the second person ("you") and the active voice.
- **Be inclusive**: use neutral language and avoid jargon, slang and metaphors that do not translate.

## Capitalization and punctuation

- **Sentence case** everywhere: headings, buttons, labels and menus ("Create account", not "Create Account").
- **No period** in headings, labels, buttons and menu items.
- **Period** in complete sentences and in descriptions of more than one sentence.
- Avoid exclamation points. Save them for truly special moments.
- Avoid all caps in sentences. The [Heading](../../components/typography/heading/index.mdx) `overtitle` is the design exception.

## Buttons and actions

The label is a **verb in the imperative** that says the result: "Save changes", "Delete project", "Send invite".

- Use **up to three words**.
- Name what will be affected in destructive actions ("Delete 3 files").
- Avoid "OK", "Yes", "No" and "Click here".

See [Button](../../components/actionable/button/usage.mdx).

## Labels, hints and placeholders

- **Label**: a short noun that names the field ("Full name"). Always visible.
- **Hint**: guidance that prevents the error ("Use 8 or more characters").
- **Placeholder**: at most a format example, never the label.

See [Input](../../components/form-and-options/input/usage.mdx).

## Error messages

A good error message answers three questions: **what happened**, **why** and **how to fix it**.

| Avoid | Prefer |
| --- | --- |
| "Invalid input." | "Enter a valid email, like name@company.com." |
| "Error 500." | "Couldn't load the data. Try again in a moment." |
| "You got the password wrong." | "The password is incorrect. Try again or reset your password." |

Do not blame the person, and do not use technical terms as the only explanation.

## Empty states

Explain **why it is empty** and **what to do next**. See [Empty State](../../components/content/empty-state/usage.mdx).

## Confirmations and notifications

- State the **result** after the action: "Invite sent to ana@company.com".
- Use the tense of the result: "File deleted", not "Deleting file".
- Keep temporary messages short (see [Toast](../../components/content/toast/usage.mdx)).

## Numbers, dates and units

- Follow the format of the person's **language and region**.
- Show **units** next to the value ("12 MB", "$29.90") or use prefixes and suffixes in the field.
- Prefer spelled-out dates in contexts that can be confusing ("October 5, 2026").

## Languages

The texts components generate on their own, such as "Close", "Show password" or "Page 3 of 20", are **in English by default**. All of them can be replaced through an attribute. Set these texts in the product's language and check each one when you use the component.

Leave room for longer text: translations often take up 30% to 50% more space.

## Inclusive language

- Use gender-neutral terms (such as "they" or "person") when the person's gender does not matter.
- Avoid expressions that exclude or stereotype.
- Describe what the image communicates in the alternative text, without assuming what people look like.
