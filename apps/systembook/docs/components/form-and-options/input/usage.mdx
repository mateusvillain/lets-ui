## Labels

Every field needs a **visible label**. It says what to type and stays visible while the person writes.

<DosDonts variant="do" title="Label above the field, always visible">
  "Email" stays in place the whole time. The person never forgets what the field asks for.
</DosDonts>

<DosDonts variant="dont" title="Placeholder instead of a label">
  The placeholder disappears when typing, has low contrast and is not a reliable name for screen readers. Use it, at most, as a format example.
</DosDonts>

- Write **short** labels, with nouns: "Full name", "ZIP code".
- Sentence case and no colon at the end.

## Hints

Use the hint to say **what the person needs to know before making a mistake**: format, limits, why you ask for the data. Example: "Use 8 or more characters, with letters and numbers."

Prefer the hint to instructions inside the placeholder.

## Required or optional

Mark what is **optional** with "(optional)", instead of marking what is required with an asterisk. In most forms, most fields are required, and the notice only needs to appear on the exception.

## Error messages

A good message says **what happened and how to fix it**, in a neutral tone and without blaming the person.

<DosDonts variant="do" title="Specific and actionable">
  "Enter a valid email, such as name@company.com."
</DosDonts>

<DosDonts variant="dont" title="Vague or accusatory">
  "Invalid input." or "You got the email wrong." They do not explain what to fix.
</DosDonts>

- Show the error **next to the field**, after the person leaves it or tries to submit. Not before they finish typing.
- Red must never be the only signal: the text message is essential.
- If there are several errors, list a summary at the top of the form and keep each message on its field.

## Field width

Fit the width to the expected content. A ZIP code does not need the width of an address. This helps the person anticipate the length of the answer.

## Prefix and suffix

Use them for fixed units and symbols ("$", "kg", "@"). That way the person does not need to type them, and the value stays clean.

## Best practices

- Use the **right type** (`email`, `tel`, `number`): the mobile keyboard adapts and validation improves.
- Do not block pasting into fields, especially passwords.
- Accept varied formats (spaces, periods, hyphens) and normalize them afterwards, instead of rejecting them.
- One form at a time: group related fields and keep a single column.
