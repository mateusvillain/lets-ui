## Labels

- Use **short nouns**, one or two words long: "General", "Security", "Billing".
- Sentence case, no punctuation.
- Labels should be **parallel** and mutually exclusive. No one should wonder which tab holds something.

<DosDonts variant="do" title="Short, distinct labels">
  "Overview", "Activity", "Settings". Each tab has a clear scope.
</DosDonts>

<DosDonts variant="dont" title="Long or overlapping labels">
  "General account information" next to "Account data". The person has to open both to know the difference.
</DosDonts>

## Number and order

- Use **two to seven** labels. With many, consider a side menu.
- Place the most used tab **first**, and it comes selected by default.
- Keep the **same order** across the application for the same content.

## Behavior

- Switching tabs **does not lose** what the person already typed in other tabs.
- The content changes in place, without reloading the page.
- Do not use nested tabs. Two levels of tabs are confusing.
- Avoid disabled tabs. If the content does not exist, hide the tab or explain why.

## Line or Card?

Use **Line** when the tabs organize the page and **Card** when they control a component inside a card. Do not mix the two styles on the same screen without a good reason.

## Best practices

- If the tabs lead to different URLs, treat them as navigation, with links.
- On narrow screens, use `expand` or let the tabs scroll horizontally.
- Show a clear indication (and not just color) of which tab is active.
