## Name the steps

- Use **short nouns** that say what happens in the step: "Account", "Address", "Payment".
- Use the description for a detail that helps decide, such as "Card or PIX", not to repeat the label.
- Keep the labels parallel: all nouns, all the same length, more or less.

<DosDonts variant="do" title="Short, parallel labels">
  "Account", "Address", "Payment", "Review".
</DosDonts>

<DosDonts variant="dont" title="Instructions as labels">
  "Fill in your personal data", "Now tell us where you live". Long labels break the row and repeat what the page already says.
</DosDonts>

## Number of steps

- Keep between **three and six** steps. Fewer than three does not need a stepper; more than six makes the flow look long and the row tight.
- Group small steps into one. A step with a single field is rarely worth it.
- Do not add or remove steps in the middle of the flow: the total the person saw at the start must hold.

## Free or linear

- By default, the person can jump to any step. Use it when the steps are independent, such as the sections of a profile.
- Use `linear` when each step depends on the previous one, such as a checkout. The person goes back to fix something, and moves forward through the button of the step.

## Moving between steps

- Keep a "Continue" or "Next" button in the content of the step. The stepper indicates progress; the main action stays in the form.
- Validate the step before advancing. If it is invalid, cancel the change and show the errors.
- Do not lose what was typed when the person goes back.
- When the step changes the content of the page, update the page title and move focus to the heading of the new step.

## Placement

- Put the horizontal stepper at the top of the flow, above the content of the step.
- Put the vertical stepper beside the content, on wide screens, or when the labels are long.
- Use one stepper per flow.
