## Follow the final layout

The skeleton is a promise of what is coming. Keep the same positions, sizes and spacing as the real content, so nothing moves when it arrives.

<DosDonts variant="do" title="Same shape as the content">
  A card with a cover and three lines, where the real card has an image, a title and a two-line summary.
</DosDonts>

<DosDonts variant="dont" title="A generic block">
  One big rectangle in place of a whole list. When the items arrive, the page jumps anyway.
</DosDonts>

## Suggest real text

- Use one bone per line, not one tall block for a paragraph.
- Vary the widths and shorten the last line (`width="56%"`), as real text does.
- Give headings a shorter, isolated line.

## Announce the loading once

Each bone is decorative. The region that will be replaced by the content announces the loading, with `role="status"`, `aria-busy="true"` and an `aria-label` such as "Loading profile". When the content enters, remove `aria-busy`.

Pass `label` to a Skeleton only when it stands for the whole region on its own.

## How long to show it

- Do not show a skeleton for very fast responses: a flash of placeholders is more distracting than a short wait.
- Do not keep it forever. If the request fails, replace it with a message and a way to try again.
- Load the sections independently when you can, so the content that is ready appears first.

## Best practices

- Do not mix a skeleton and a spinner for the same content.
- Do not use skeletons for content that the person is not waiting for, such as a hidden menu.
- Keep the composition simple: a few shapes are enough to suggest the layout.
