## Purpose

Most of an interface is text. Typography decides **what the person reads first**, how much effort reading takes and whether the screen feels like the same product as the previous one.

That is why the system does not offer loose sizes and weights to combine: it offers **composite scales**, each with a role. You choose the role ("section heading"), and the scale brings the rest.

## Principles

- **Clear hierarchy.** Size and weight say what matters most. Three or four levels per screen are enough.
- **Readability first.** Lines of 45 to 75 characters, comfortable line height and enough contrast.
- **One role, one scale.** The same type of content always uses the same scale, on any screen.
- **Fluid by default.** Headings grow with the screen continuously, without jumps at breakpoints.
- **Native.** The default family is the operating system font: zero download, familiar reading.

## Family

The system has two family roles, **heading** and **body**, defined by the brand. In the `lets-ui` brand, both use the **system font stack**: San Francisco on macOS and iOS, Segoe UI on Windows, Roboto on Android and the native fonts on Linux.

| Role | Used by | Fallback |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `heading` | `display`, `title`, `headline`, `subtitle`, `block-title`, `subheadline`, `overtitle` | `Helvetica Neue`, `Arial`, `sans-serif` and the emoji fonts |
| `body` | `body--lg`, `body--md`, `body--sm` | The same |

A brand can change the heading family without touching the body one, and vice versa. The stack always ends in a generic family (`sans-serif`), so no text is left without a font.

## Type scale

| Role | Scale | Family | Size | Weight | Line height | Case | Usage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Display | `display` | heading | `3xl` | Light (300) | 1.2 | — | Hero, marketing highlight. |
| Heading | `title` | heading | `2xl` | Semibold (600) | 1.2 | — | Page title. One per page. |
| Heading | `headline` | heading | `1xl` | Semibold (600) | 1.2 | — | Section heading. |
| Heading | `subtitle` | heading | `lg` | Regular (400) | 1.2 | — | Subtitle, introduction paragraph. |
| Title | `block-title` | heading | `md` | Medium (500) | 1.2 | — | Card, modal or block title. |
| Title | `subheadline` | heading | `sm` | Regular (400) | 1.2 | — | Smaller group title. |
| Label | `overtitle` | heading | `3xs` | Semibold (600) | 1.2 | UPPERCASE | Label above a heading. |
| Body | `body--lg` | body | `1xs` | Regular (400) | 1.5 | — | Default running text. |
| Body / Label | `body--md` | body | `2xs` | Regular (400) | 1.5 | — | Secondary text, labels of `md` fields. |
| Caption | `body--sm` | body | `3xs` | Regular (400) | 1.5 | — | Caption, note, helper text. |

Each scale is applied by the class of the same name (`.headline`), by the `font-scale(headline)` mixin in components, or by the [Heading](../../components/typography/heading/index.mdx) and [Body](../../components/typography/body/index.mdx) components.

```html
<lui-heading variant="title" label="Account settings"></lui-heading>
<p class="body--lg">Update your details and preferences.</p>
```

```scss
.panel__title {
  @include font-scale(block-title);
  color: text(heading);
}
```

## Sizes

Up to `2xl`, the scale follows a ratio of **1.2** (minor third) from 16px, measured on the maximum values; `3xl` takes a bigger jump, reserved for display. The three smallest are fixed; from `sm` up, sizes are **fluid**: they grow continuously between a **320px** and a **1440px** viewport, and stop at the extremes.

| Token | CSS | At 320px | At 1440px |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `3xs` | `--lui-brand-typography-font-size-3xs` | 11.1px | 11.1px |
| `2xs` | `--lui-brand-typography-font-size-2xs` | 13.3px | 13.3px |
| `1xs` | `--lui-brand-typography-font-size-1xs` | 16px | 16px |
| `sm` | `--lui-brand-typography-font-size-sm` | 16.8px | 19.2px |
| `md` | `--lui-brand-typography-font-size-md` | 17.7px | 23px |
| `lg` | `--lui-brand-typography-font-size-lg` | 19.9px | 27.6px |
| `1xl` | `--lui-brand-typography-font-size-1xl` | 22.4px | 33.2px |
| `2xl` | `--lui-brand-typography-font-size-2xl` | 25.2px | 39.8px |
| `3xl` | `--lui-brand-typography-font-size-3xl` | 35.9px | 68.8px |

The pixel values assume the browser's default font size (16px). Since the extremes are in `rem`, **everything grows** when the person increases the browser font size.

## Line height, weight and spacing

### Line height

Headings use **1.2**: short lines, at large sizes, look loose with more. Body uses **1.5**, the comfortable minimum for paragraphs.

## Hierarchy

A page needs a clear **reading path**: the eye must know where to start, where to continue and what it can skip.

1. **One** `title` per page, saying where the person is.
2. `headline` for each section, and `block-title` for blocks and cards within it.
3. `body--lg` for the content, `body--md` and `body--sm` for what supports it.
4. `overtitle` and `subtitle` are accessories of the title: they appear above or below it, never alone.

The visual order must match the order of the heading elements in the HTML (`h1`, `h2`, `h3`). The scale is visual; the semantic level is chosen separately, through the Heading's `as` attribute.

## Content rules

### Long text

- Limit the line to **45–75 characters** (about `65ch`). Longer lines make the eye lose its place when returning.
- Align left in left-to-right languages. Justified text creates gaps between words.
- Separate paragraphs with space, not with indentation.

### Labels

- Field and button labels use `body--md` or `body--lg`, depending on the component size.
- Sentence case, no period. See [Writing](../content/writing.mdx).
- Uppercase is restricted to `overtitle`.

### Numbers

- In tables and values that are compared, use fixed-width numerals (`font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`) and right-align the column.
- Show the unit next to the value and follow the format of the person's language.

### Truncated content

- Prefer **wrapping** the line to truncating it. Cut-off text hides information.
- When truncating is unavoidable, use the Heading's or Body's `line-clamp` attribute and make sure the full text is available elsewhere (a tooltip, a detail page).
- Never truncate button labels, error messages or the name of something the person needs to identify.

## Responsiveness

The system uses **fluid sizes** instead of per-breakpoint variants. From `sm` to `3xl`, each token is a `clamp()` that grows linearly between 320px and 1440px wide. The result:

- Large headings shrink considerably on mobile (`3xl` goes from 68.8px to 35.9px), and the body barely changes.
- There is no size jump when crossing a breakpoint.
- You do not need to write any media query for typography.

## Accessibility

- **Minimum size.** The default body is 16px. `body--sm` (11.1px) and `overtitle` are for short, secondary text, never for content the person needs to read to complete a task.
- **200% zoom.** Sizes are in `rem` and respect the font chosen in the browser. The fluid `vw` component is small enough not to prevent zoom (WCAG 1.4.4). Test it: nothing can be cut off or overlap.
- **Line height.** The body uses 1.5, the value WCAG 1.4.12 asks the layout to support. Do not reduce it.
- **Contrast.** `text.body` and `text.heading` pass AAA in both themes. `text.caption` passes AA, with little margin: do not use it on colored backgrounds. See [Color](color.mdx).
- **Structure.** Use real heading elements (`h1`–`h6`), in order, without skipping levels. Screen readers navigate by them.
- **Uppercase** makes reading harder, and some screen readers spell out short words in capitals. Restrict it to `overtitle` and write the text in normal case: the transformation is done by CSS.

## Best practices

- Choose the scale by the text's **role**, not by the size that "looks good".
- Keep the semantic level (`h2`) and the visual scale (`headline`) consistent across the product.
- Prefer **weight** to **color** or **uppercase** for emphasis within a paragraph.
- Use `text.caption` for supporting information and `text.body` for the rest.
- Test headings with the longest possible text, and in other languages: translations take up 30% to 50% more space.

## Anti-patterns

- **Loose size and weight.** `font-size: 18px; font-weight: 600` creates a parallel scale that follows neither the brand nor the fluidity.
- **Choosing `h1`–`h6` by size.** An `h4` because "the `h2` is too big" breaks heading navigation. Choose the right level and change the scale.
- **Text in images.** It does not scale, does not translate and is not read by screen readers.
- **Paragraphs in `body--sm`.** Small text to "fit more" trades readability for density.
- **Typed uppercase.** "SAVE" in the content is read letter by letter by some screen readers. Use the `overtitle` scale, which transforms the text through CSS.
