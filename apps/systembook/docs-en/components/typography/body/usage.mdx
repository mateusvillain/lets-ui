## Readability

- Keep lines between **45 and 75 characters**. Long lines are tiring and make the person lose their place when going back to the start of the next one.
- Use **short paragraphs**, three to five lines, and leave space between them.
- **Align left** (in left-to-right languages). Justified text creates uneven gaps that make reading harder, especially for people with dyslexia.

<DosDonts variant="do" title="Left alignment and short lines">
  A block with a comfortable width and a ragged right edge. Reading flows.
</DosDonts>

<DosDonts variant="dont" title="Justified, wide text">
  A column that takes the whole screen and is justified. The spaces between words vary and are tiring.
</DosDonts>

## Sizes

- `md` is the default for content.
- `lg` for a page's opening paragraph.
- `sm` for captions and notes. Avoid using `sm` for important content.

## Style

- Use **bold** and *italic* sparingly, for real emphasis.
- Avoid underline outside links: it suggests a click.
- Do not use all caps in long text.

## Writing

- Prefer **short sentences and plain language**.
- Start with the most important information.
- Avoid jargon and unexplained acronyms.

## Best practices

- Keep the same size for the same type of content.
- Ensure a contrast of at least 4.5:1.
- Test with text enlarged to 200%: the layout must not break.
