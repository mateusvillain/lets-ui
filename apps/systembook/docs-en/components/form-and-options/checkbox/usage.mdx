## Checkbox, Radio or Switch?

| Situation | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Check **several** independent items | Checkbox |
| Choose **just one** option among several | Radio Group |
| Turn something on or off **right away** | Switch |
| Accept terms when submitting a form | Checkbox |

## Labels

- The label should be **affirmative** and describe the option: "Receive updates by email".
- Avoid negatives and double negatives: "I don't want to not receive…".
- Keep the label short. If you need to explain, add helper text below it.
- The whole label is clickable. Do not put links inside it unless it is essential (as in "I have read the [terms]").

<DosDonts variant="do" title="Affirmative, specific label">
  "Receive a weekly summary by email". The person knows what happens when they check it.
</DosDonts>

<DosDonts variant="dont" title="Negative or ambiguous label">
  "Don't send promotional emails". Checking it means the opposite of what seems desirable, and it confuses.
</DosDonts>

## Checkbox lists

- Align the boxes **vertically**, one per line. Side by side, the boundary between options is hard to tell.
- Order them logically: by most common use, alphabetically or by natural sequence.
- Use a title for the group (such as a `<fieldset>` with a `<legend>`), explaining what the person is choosing.
- To check them all, offer a "Select all" box at the top.

## When the choice is required

Use `required` for options that must be checked (such as accepting the terms). Show the error message **close to the box**, saying what to do: "Accept the terms to continue."

## Best practices

- Do not check options by default when they benefit the company (such as receiving promotions). Let the person decide.
- Do not use a checkbox for immediate actions: it suggests "save later".
- Keep a comfortable touch area: clicking should also work on the label.
