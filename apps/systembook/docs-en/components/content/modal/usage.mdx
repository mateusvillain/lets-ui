---
title: "Usage"
---

## Use sparingly

The Modal **interrupts** the person. Every use costs attention. Before creating one, ask whether the information could appear on the page itself.

## Title and content

- The title says **what is being asked**, as a question or an action: "Delete this project?"
- The body explains the consequence in a few lines.
- Avoid scrolling. If the content is long, use a page or a Drawer.

<DosDonts variant="do" title="Clear consequence">
  "Delete the project 'New website'? This removes 24 files and cannot be undone."
</DosDonts>

<DosDonts variant="dont" title="Vague question">
  "Are you sure?" Without saying about what, the person has to guess what the button does.
</DosDonts>

## Buttons

- Place the **primary action on the right** and the secondary one ("Cancel") next to it.
- Use **specific verbs** on the buttons: "Delete project" and "Cancel", instead of "Yes" and "No".
- In destructive actions, use the `danger` button and do not leave it with initial focus by mistake.
- Always offer a clear way out.

## Opening and closing

- Open the Modal **only from an action by the person**, never on page load.
- Always offer the close button and allow `Esc`.
- Do not stack one Modal on top of another.

## Content and forms

In short forms, put focus on the first field. Validate on submit and keep the Modal open if there is an error, showing the messages.

## Best practices

- Always give the Modal a title. It is the name screen readers announce.
- Keep the size consistent across similar Modals.
- Never use a Modal for advertising or notices that do not require a decision.
