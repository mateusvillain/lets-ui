title: "Accessibility"
---

## Keyboard

The Avatar is **not interactive**: it does not receive focus or respond to the keyboard. To open a profile or a menu, wrap it in a button or link, with its own accessible name and focus ring.

## Screen reader

- With `name` or `status`, the Avatar is announced as an **image**, with the full name and the status ("Mateus Villain, Online"), not the initials letter by letter.
- Without a name and without a status, it is **decorative** and stays hidden from assistive technology. That is the case of an avatar next to the written name.
- Initials, photo and status dot stay out of the accessibility tree, because the name is already on the avatar.
- The default status texts are in English. Change them with `status-label`.

## Visual

- The initials have a contrast of at least 4.5:1 against the background in every color.
- The status has its **own shape** in addition to color: online filled, away with clock hands, busy with a bar, offline with an empty center.
- In high contrast mode, the avatar gets a border.

## Known limitation

The status dot does not reach 3:1 contrast against the ring around it (from 1.6:1 to 2.6:1), below what WCAG 1.4.11 requires for graphical elements that carry information. The text in the accessible name compensates for screen readers, but not for visual reading. The fix is to use a darker shade in the status colors.

## For designers

- Always set the name when the avatar is the only identification of the person.
- Do not use the avatar color to communicate anything.
- Give avatar groups an accessible name.
